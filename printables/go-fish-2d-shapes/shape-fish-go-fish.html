Shape Fish a 2D shapes Go Fish game

What's in the deck

52 cards: 13 shapes, with 4 cards of each. Every shape has one card of each colour:

  • Red: the plain shape, with its sides & vertices
  • Green: a real-life object, with its lines of symmetry
  • Blue: a real-life object, with its angles
  • Yellow: a real-life object, with its parallel & curved sides

A set is all 4 cards of one shape (red, green, blue and yellow).

How to play

  1. Shuffle. Deal 7 cards each (2 players) or 5 each (3+ players). Spread the rest face down as the pond.
  2. On your turn, ask one player for a shape you already hold: "Have you got any hexagons?"
  3. If they have any, they hand them all over and you go again. If not, they say "Go fish!" and you take a card from the pond.
  4. When you collect all 4 cards of a shape, lay the set down and read the 4 facts out loud.
  5. The game ends when all 13 sets are made. Most sets wins!

Two levels, one game

Level 1: ask by name

"Have you got any circles?"
Great for learning shape names and spotting shapes in real life. When you lay down a set, just read out the red card (sides & corners).

Level 2: ask by property

Ask using a fact instead of the name:
"Have you got a shape with 6 vertices?"
"…with 4 right angles?" (careful, square and rectangle!)
"…with 1 line of symmetry?"
If more than one shape fits, the other player chooses which one to give.

Bonus challenges

• Before you lay a set, predict one fact, then check the card.
• Find something in the house that matches a card.
• Use a mirror to test the lines of symmetry.

Printing

Print on card at 100% / actual size (A4). Pages 3 to 8 are the fronts. Page 9 is the card backs: print it on the reverse of each front page if you like, then cut along the grey lines. Laminate for long life.

Words to know. Vertex (plural vertices): a corner where two sides meet. Right angle: a square corner, 90°. Acute: smaller than a right angle. Obtuse: bigger than a right angle but less than a straight line. Reflex: bigger than a straight line (180°). Parallel: lines that always stay the same distance apart, like train tracks. Regular: all sides and all angles are equal.
